# 01 — Strategy: how this works better than YouTube

## The problem with "just watch YouTube"

YouTube creators show every new AI tool the week it comes out. That is useful, but a learner who only watches gets:

| What YouTube gives | What learners actually need |
|---|---|
| Random videos in random order | A path: what to learn first, second, third |
| Mostly English (or Hindi/Urdu) | Their own language: Pashto or Dari |
| Watching, not doing | Practice tasks with feedback |
| No proof they learned anything | A certificate an employer or client can verify |
| Learning alone | A class, a group, a mentor |
| Tool news that is old in 3 months | Skills that last (prompting, automation, thinking) plus fresh updates |
| Needs fast internet | Lessons that work on slow data and offline |
| Nothing after the video | Help finding clients, freelance work and jobs |

**Your positioning, in one line:**
> YouTube shows you what is new. We teach you, step by step in your own language, how to use it and get paid for it, and give you a certificate to prove it.

## Do not compete with YouTube; use it

YouTube, Telegram and Facebook are your **free school**, and the LMS is your **paid institute**.

```
Free (YouTube / TikTok / Facebook / Telegram)          Paid (your LMS)
──────────────────────────────────────────────         ─────────────────────────────
"AI news this week" in Pashto/Dari            ──►     Structured courses, Levels 2–5
Short tricks, tool demos                      ──►     Practice tasks + graded projects
Full free Level 0–1 lessons                   ──►     Exams + verifiable certificates
Live Q&A                                      ──►     Mentors, study circles, AI tutor
Student success stories                       ──►     Freelance / job support
```

Every free video ends with one call to action: *"The full course, with exercises and a certificate, is free/paid on [your site]."*

You can also become **the** Pashto/Dari channel that shows every new AI tool. Few people do that in these languages today, so that space is open to you.

## The three things you sell

1. **The Institute (your own courses):** the 6-level ladder and the industry tracks. You control quality. This is where you start.
2. **The Marketplace (other teachers' courses):** from month 6+, vetted Afghan instructors publish courses on your platform, and you take a share. This grows the catalog without you making every course.
3. **Programs (university-style):** bundles of courses into diplomas ("AI Professional Diploma, 6 months"), with cohorts, deadlines, mentors and a final project. These are the highest price and value.

Do not call it a "university" or say it issues "degrees" until you are accredited (see file 10). Use **Institute / Academy** and **Certificate / Diploma / Professional Certificate**.

## What makes it defensible

- **Language moat:** a full Pashto + Dari AI glossary and curriculum takes a year to build. Nobody else will copy that quickly.
- **Certificates with verification:** they become a credential people show on LinkedIn, Upwork and in job applications.
- **Community + mentors:** graduates help new students, which keeps people coming back.
- **Offline + low data:** you serve students the big platforms ignore.
- **Your own platform:** you own the students, the data and the payments, not YouTube or Udemy.

## Rules that keep it working

1. Build narrow and deep first: Levels 0–2 in both languages before anything else.
2. Every course has practice and a project, never just videos.
3. A native speaker reviews every lesson.
4. Free must be genuinely good. That is your marketing.
5. Measure completion rate, not just sign-ups (file 19).
